**Position score by issue**

- **Idea:** for each issue, show where the politician stands as a bar running from conservative on one side to liberal on the other. The center line means a mixed record.
- **How to read it:** a longer bar means a more consistent record on that side. A short bar near the center means they've voted both ways.
- **Based on:** their record on each issue: votes, sponsored bills and official acts, with statements counting half (Rule 1). It's the same evidence Your match uses.
- **Issues:**
  - Immigration
  - Taxes and government spending
  - Civil liberties: privacy, surveillance, free speech
  - Environment and energy
  - Health care
  - Abortion and reproductive rights
  - Guns
  - Workers and wages
  - Trade and tariffs
  - Foreign policy and defense
  - Voting and elections
  - Education
  - Criminal justice and policing
  - LGBTQ rights
  - Housing
  - Social Security and Medicare
- **Open question:** how many votes or bills on an issue are needed before it gets a bar.

**Changes I made to your wording**
- **"Liberal" instead of "positive":** labeling one side positive makes it look like the better side. "Conservative" and "liberal" keep the bar neutral.
- **A center line for "mixed":** this shows the difference between someone in the middle and someone with no record.
- **The added issues mostly match ones the site already tracks in Your match,** like health coverage, abortion, guns, labor, tariffs, Ukraine aid, voter ID and private-school funding. Most of the data is already there. The new ones are criminal justice, LGBTQ rights, housing, and Social Security and Medicare.
